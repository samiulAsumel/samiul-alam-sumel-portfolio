Product case study · Building

OpsFlow ◐In development

A connected operations system for small and mid-sized transport and logistics businesses.

OpsFlow is designed to replace scattered Excel sheets, WhatsApp threads, notebooks and disconnected records with one connected operational workflow, from customer orders to trips, invoices, payments and outstanding balances.

View OpsFlow early build ↗Have a similar workflow?

The early build is a sign-in shell of the current software. Use synthetic data only.

The problem

Information scattered across too many places

Many small and mid-sized businesses may manage these workflows across a combination of spreadsheets, messaging and manual records. That can make it harder to keep one consistent view of customers, orders, trips, invoices, payments, outstanding balances and reports.

Who it is for: small and mid-sized transport, logistics, freight, warehousing and distribution businesses.

A typical pattern, not a claim about every business:

  • Customer information→Excel
  • Orders→WhatsApp / phone
  • Trips→Notebook / spreadsheet
  • Invoices→Separate files
  • Payments→Manual records
  • Outstanding→Calculated by hand
  • Reports→Spreadsheet work

Multi-tenant architecture

Tenant isolation: separate company environments

Company AIsolated business data
Company BIsolated business data
Company CIsolated business data

The product is designed around company-level data isolation, so several businesses can use the same application without mixing operational records.

●Implemented Implemented in the foundation and the existing modules: every tenant record carries an organisation id, tenant identity comes only from the signed-in session, queries are scoped to it, and composite foreign keys enforce it in the database. An end-to-end test suite exercises it. Modules still to be built will follow the same rule.

Product thinking

From internal tool to general product

My production systems

Each began with one specific operational problem: a billing calculation, a vehicle balance, an overtime bill. They solve that workflow directly.

OpsFlow

Instead of one port workflow, it models the patterns that recur across transport and logistics businesses: customers, orders, trips, invoices and payments.

Why it exists

Why I started building OpsFlow

After working with real operational workflows, I saw how much business information can become fragmented when orders, operations, billing and payments are managed separately. OpsFlow is my attempt to turn that understanding into a reusable product for businesses with similar operational complexity.

  • SimpleKeep daily operational tasks understandable.
  • ConnectedConnect business events instead of creating isolated records.
  • PracticalPrioritise useful workflows over unnecessary complexity.
  • TraceableMake important operational and financial information easier to follow.
  • ExtensibleCreate a foundation that can grow with the business.

These are design principles, not claims about results. There has been no market validation.
