01Business problem

Preparing an overtime bill was a repetitive, multi-step manual calculation: find the employee’s pay, work out an hourly rate, add up the hours day by day, then produce the bill in the required format. Errors were easy to make and hard to spot.

This is overtime billing, not payroll: the system prepares one kind of bill from a small set of inputs.

02Existing workflow

The bill is derived from two inputs: the employee’s profile and the hours worked each day.

  1. Operational inputOvertime worked, recorded per day.
  2. OT / bill dataEmployee profile plus daily from / to hours.
  3. Business rulesHourly rate is basic pay divided by 104, rounded up.
  4. CalculationCumulative day-by-day hours multiplied by the rate.
  5. RecordA saved bill per employee per month.
  6. ReportA4 print, an all-bills view and a dashboard.
  1. Manual multi-step processFind the pay, work out the rate, add the hours by hand.
  2. Single data-entry workflowEmployee profile once, hours per day.
  3. Automated calculationRate, cumulative total and amount in words.
  4. A4 billPrint-ready output.

The software is built around the process, not the other way around. Designed to reduce repetitive spreadsheet-based work.

03My approach

I encoded the rate rule once, at the employee-profile level, and made daily hours the only thing entered per bill. Everything else, from the hourly rate to the cumulative total and the amount in words, is derived, so the bill cannot disagree with its inputs.

  1. Real operational workflowHow overtime bills were prepared.
  2. Business rulesRate derivation and cumulative hours.
  3. Structured dataEmployee profiles, dated hour entries, bills.
  4. Calculation logicRate, totals and amount in words.
  5. Web interfaceThree-step bill flow, Bengali-language UI.
  6. ReportsA4 bill, all-bills list, dashboard.

04System design

Employee and bill records live in Firestore behind sign-in, with a local-storage fallback so the tool still works if the connection drops.

  1. UserAdmin or employee, signing in with an ID.
  2. Web interfaceBengali-language, three-step bill flow.
  3. Business rules and calculationHourly rate and cumulative totals, in the browser.
  4. Firebase Authentication and Cloud FirestoreAccounts, employee records and bills, with a local-storage fallback.
  5. OutputPrint-ready A4 bill.
