Engineering / How I Build

How I Build

“

A job starts with a conversation, not a keyboard. I ask what the workflow looks like today, where people lose time to a manual step, and what "done" needs to mean for them — then I write that down as a plain-language requirement and confirm it back before any code gets written. From there, every project on this site was built with AI assistance. That's a plain fact, not a confession — Claude and similar tools are part of how I get from a requirement to working code. But using a tool isn't a skill. The skill is the workflow around it: getting the requirement right, testing what the tool produces, and being able to explain every part of it clearly.

Working From Requirements
Talk First, Build Second

Before any design or code, I go through what the person or team actually needs — what the current process looks like, who touches it, and where it breaks down. That conversation becomes the spec.

Automate The Manual Step

For a company or industry client, the goal is almost always the same: find the part of their running process still done by hand — a paper form, a shared Excel sheet, a recalculation someone repeats every day — and replace it with a deployed tool the whole team can use. That's exactly what portbill, otbill, and carview do for port operations at Mongla; the same approach applies to any workflow with a manual bottleneck.

Solve The Problem, Not Just The Request

A website or dashboard is the deliverable, but the actual job is closing the gap the client described — if the stated request doesn't fully solve it, I say so before building it anyway.

From Problem to Product
↓ ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓

01 · Build, Refine, Explain

A piece gets built with AI assistance, then manually tested against the actual business rules and edge cases — refined until it's right, not just running. Then I explain it clearly, in the README and out loud, as if teaching a junior developer. That's what makes it mine to ship.

02 · Context Engineering

"Build me a billing app" gets a weak result from any model. Business rules, the real data model, edge cases, and acceptance criteria stated up front get an accurate one — I treat specifying that context as its own deliberate skill, not an afterthought.
